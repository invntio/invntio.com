# Pricing models for ongoing hosting and maintenance

Research date: 2026-10-19. Question: for a one-person studio (Invntio, Pennsylvania) serving US and Dominican Republic / LatAm small businesses, which model should ongoing hosting and maintenance use?

- **Model A:** monthly plans with included change hours (Hosting & care $29/mo with no changes; Maintenance $149/mo with 2 h; Plus $349/mo with 5 h; extra $85/h).
- **Model B:** hosting fee ($29/mo) plus prepaid hour blocks valid 90 days (5 h $425, 10 h $750; loose hours $95/h).

Method: public pricing pages, marketplace listings and published surveys only. No provider was contacted. Some figures come from search-engine snippets of pages that could not be fetched directly; those are marked *(snippet)*. Many sources are vendor marketing, so treat prices as published asks, not what clients actually pay. Lines marked **Inference** are our reasoning, not a finding of the source.

Currency conversions are approximate (Oct 2026): 1 USD ≈ RD$60, MXN 18, COP 4,000, PEN 3.7.

**Finding:** a recurring monthly fee is the dominant model in every segment (34 of 43 providers, 79%; 14 of 17 US agencies, 10 of 12 freelancers, 11 of 14 LatAm). Within monthly plans, US agencies mostly include hours; LatAm providers mostly sell a flat care/hosting fee and quote changes separately. Pure prepaid blocks are a minority (5 of 43), plus 3 hybrids that sell blocks next to monthly plans. Where block expiry is published it is none (Sumy, Shero) or 6–12 months (Freelock, Cre8d). Only one provider in the sample uses a 90-day-style expiry: EYMPro in the DR (3 months).

Survey support for the same picture:

- ManageWP 2020 survey (2,279 respondents, 51% freelancers, 28% agency owners): 85% offer WordPress maintenance; 73.4% offer multiple tiers, most often three; 54.3% of highest tiers include development hours. ([ManageWP 2020](https://managewp.com/2020-wordpress-maintenance-survey-results-services-fees/))
- ManageWP 2022: 85.3% offer maintenance; 52.3% offer 3+ tiers. ([ManageWP 2022](https://managewp.com/2022-wordpress-maintenance-survey-results/), *snippet*)
- Mexican price guides describe the common forms as "planes mensuales fijos o costo por hora", with prepaid "bonos" of hours as a discount variant. ([Cronoshare MX](https://www.cronoshare.com.mx/cuanto-cuesta/mantener-pagina-web))
- DR guide: providers charge a monthly plan, per hour for larger changes, or per update; it does not mention hour packs. ([Pixel Caribe](https://pixelcaribe.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-sitio-web-republica-dominicana/))

**Inference, placing Invntio's numbers:**

- US: $29 hosting & care is below the cheapest US care plans ($39–99) and close to what Rocket.net quotes an agency owner who "effectively worked for free" at $29. $149 with 2 h and $349 with 5 h are below FatLab/Bellano/Codeable for the same hours. Extra $85/h is below every US agency overage in the sample ($100–225). Effective rate of Model A after subtracting hosting: ($149 − $29) / 2 h = $60/h; ($349 − $29) / 5 h = $64/h.
- DR/LatAm: $29 is at the top of hosting-plus-care offers (Intelia $19–77, VIOLET $50 care) and fine. $149 is upper-mid for the DR (RD$2,500–12,000 ≈ $40–200) and $349 is above almost all published LatAm SMB plans. $85–95/h is above Mexican rates and at the high end of DR "major change" rates.
- Model B's $85/h (5 h) and $75/h (10 h) are higher per hour than Model A's effective $60–64/h, so B charges more per hour for flexibility, which matches how US block sellers price (volume discount, no commitment).

## 3. Evidence on what sells and retains

There is no public dataset comparing churn of monthly-with-hours plans against prepaid blocks. What exists:

- **Recurring revenue and profitability.** The Admin Bar 2026 State of the WordPress Agency (n ≈ 617, 51 countries): with 0% recurring revenue (care plans, hosting, retainers), 58.3% of agencies are rarely or never profitable; at 25–49% recurring that falls to 9.7%, and to 4.5% at 50–74%. Agencies using a retainer pricing model: 51.2% consistently profitable, 56.6% grew in 2025. ([The Admin Bar 2026](https://theadminbar.com/2026-survey/))
- **Prevalence of tiers with hours.** 54.3% of the highest maintenance tiers include development hours (ManageWP 2020, above).
- **Retainer client lifetime.** A Focus Digital 2026 report cited in partner content says retainer clients stay 56 months vs 24 for project clients ([Business Cloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/scaling-a-digital-agency-by-seamlessly-integrating-client-maintenance-services/)). Weak source: sponsored article, method not shown.
- **Share of profit.** Nimbus 2022 survey of ~200 UK agencies: retainers bring 21–30% of profit on average ([Nimbus report PDF](https://nimbushosting.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Nimbus-Agency-Retainer-Report-.pdf), *snippet*).
- **Attach rate.** No measured figure found. One agency blog sets a 60–80% attach-rate target and churn under 5%/month as goals, not measurements ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/maintenance-retainer-recurring-revenue-2026), *snippet*). The Admin Bar founder reports a self-reported 100% conversion to care plans since 2019 and says clients decline mainly because they do not see why maintenance is needed or think they can do it themselves, not because of price ([The Admin Bar post](https://theadminbar.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/4290)).
- **Hosting-to-maintenance upsell.** No public conversion data. Rocket.net's agency guidance is to make hosting, backups, updates and a monthly report "table stakes" in every tier and sell growth tiers by showing what the next tier unlocks; its survey of partner agencies gives the Starter/Growth/Premium ranges used above ([Rocket.net](https://rocket.net/blog/agency-pricing-strategies-for-hosting-and-maintenance/)).

### The "paying for hours I don't use" objection

- Retainers are "usually structured with a 'use it or lose it' mentality", and in light months unused hours "could feel like wasted money" ([CommonPlaces](https://www.commonplaces.com/?p=5551)).
- Practitioners answer it in three ways: (1) rollover with a cap (LimeLight 10% for 30 days; Antenna up to 10x; PaperStreet up to 1 year); (2) reframe the fee as priority and a lower rate, not a bucket of hours (2008 Drupal consulting list, [thread](https://lists.drupal.org/pipermail/consulting/2008-September/002567.html)); (3) right-size: LimeLight says unused hours are "rarely" an issue and moves clients to a smaller tier when they are ([LimeLight](https://limelightmarketing.com/blogs/how-website-support-retainer-hours-work/)).
- Blocks answer the objection by design: a UK agency says clients like that every paid hour is visibly accounted for, and the agency gets paid in advance ([search summary of Nebula/Antropy pages](https://nebula.design/block-hour-retainer/)). But blocks reintroduce the same issue at expiry, and published US expiry windows are 6–12 months or none (Freelock 6 mo, Cre8d 12 mo, IGV 12 mo, Sumy and Shero never). A 90-day window is shorter than any US example found; the only match is EYMPro in the DR.
- **Inference:** a $29 hosting-only tier removes the objection for clients who rarely need changes, which is what FatLab ($99, 0 h), GoWP ($39) and Bellano ($125, 0.5 h) do with their entry tiers.

## 5. Takeaways for Invntio (inference)

1. Model A matches the market norm in all three segments; Model B exists mostly as an add-on, not as the main offer.
2. A good fit is A as the main offer plus B as an option: sell blocks to clients who refuse a monthly fee, and let block hours not expire while the client keeps an active plan (Freelock pattern).
3. If blocks expire, 90 days is stricter than US norms (6–12 months). For US clients, 6 months with the clock reset on each top-up is more defensible. For DR clients, 3 months has a local precedent (EYMPro).
4. Add a capped rollover to A (for example unused hours carry one month, max one month's allotment) to answer the "hours I don't use" objection without building up a liability.
5. US prices are below market at every tier. LatAm prices for the $349 tier are above most local published plans, so price the two markets separately or lead with the $29 and $149 tiers in the DR.
